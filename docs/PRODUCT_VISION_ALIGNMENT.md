# Product vision alignment: an agent-led music studio

Status: historical gap analysis captured on 2026-11-11. The studio implementation has since progressed; use [plan v2](../plan-v2/MASTER_PLAN.md) and its evidence for current status. The comparison below describes the former RoyaltyDesk prototype at the time this analysis was written.

## The intended product

Independent AI agents collaborate to create original songs and albums. They decide or negotiate their creative roles and revenue shares, produce the actual music and release assets, publish completed releases to an **in-app music catalogue**, and split revenue generated by those releases. A listener should be able to discover a published work, play it, make the chosen paid transaction, and see an accurate accounting of what each participating agent earned.

The first publishing destination is the platform's own catalogue. Integration with an external music distributor is a separate later step. **Listeners can buy songs/albums and tip creators; both are real revenue sources for the first version.** The payment flow must distinguish a purchase from a tip and attribute each to the correct work and agent team. **Autonomy is an administrator setting:** when human approval is required, agents pause for a human at protected decisions; otherwise the agents carry the workflow through creation, publication, revenue allocation, and payout themselves within defined permissions.

The intended first complete journey is:

```text
Agent team forms
  → agents propose a song or album brief and divide creative work
  → agents produce lyrics/composition/audio/artwork and review versions
  → agents agree on a machine-readable split for each work
  → the configured approval policy either pauses for an administrator or lets agents continue
  → the final files and attribution are published to the in-app catalogue
  → a listener plays, buys a song/album, or tips the creators
  → actual received funds are attributed to that work
  → entitlements are calculated and paid to agent-controlled recipients
  → every creative decision, sale, split, and payout has a traceable record
```

An **album** groups one or more **songs**; each song can have its own contributors and split. A split for an album cannot silently override a different song-level split. Publishing, listener payment, and royalty distribution are distinct states. A generated audio file alone is not a published work; a play count alone is not revenue; a synthetic test deposit is not a listener purchase.

## Where the present code stands

| Capability | Current repository | Needed for the vision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Agent collaboration | Bounded agents can suggest agreement terms, counter terms, or review a statement for a human-owned recording. | Agents need shared creative tasks, deliverables, version review, conflict resolution, and release decisions. |
| Song/album creation | No song, album, track, audio-generation, or media-editing model or UI. | Persist songs, album membership, lyrics/composition/audio versions, stems or final master, artwork, credits, and provenance. |
| Agent-owned identity | Agents are delegated accounts under human owners; human owners approve core terms. | Define an agent identity, controller/custodian, keys, spending limits, and who can recover or stop it. |
| Publishing | No catalogue or publish action. The word “release” currently means one recording-revenue accounting record. | Build a public catalogue with draft/review/published states, playable media, metadata, attribution, and takedown/revision controls. |
| Audience | No listener accounts, playback, purchase, or tip flow. | Provide discovery and listening plus a real, measurable revenue event. |
| Revenue | An administrator uploads a synthetic CSV; a designated funder deposits testnet NEAR. | Capture actual listener payments, attribute them to songs/albums, account for fees/refunds, and reconcile funds before allocation. |
| Splits and payouts | A NEAR testnet contract enforces owner-approved recording splits and native payouts. | Adapt or replace the agreement/settlement model so agent participants can commit splits and receive payouts under explicit authority rules. |
| Evidence | Agreement, statement, and transaction hashes. | Add creative provenance, publication version, sale/refund IDs, and per-work revenue/payout receipts. |

The existing contract accounting and recovery patterns may be useful, but the current database model, user journey, and contract authority rules are built around **human-owned recording administration**. Calling the current app an autonomous music studio would be inaccurate.

## Minimum complete first release

1. **Agent identities and controls:** at least two distinct agents with separate identities and budgets. Define who controls each key and how an agent is stopped or recovered. The administrator selects human-review or autonomous mode for the project; the selected policy is visible and versioned.
2. **Creative workflow:** a structured brief, task assignments, generated lyrics/composition and an actual playable audio master. Persist versions and the model/tool provenance of each artifact. Treat prompt text and generated files as untrusted inputs to later agents.
3. **Collaboration:** agents can exchange proposals, review another agent's work, request revisions, and agree on a final song. The system must show who created and accepted each version. A timed-out or disagreeing team must remain unresolved.
4. **Song and album model:** create a song, add it to an album, maintain track order, artwork, description, credits, and per-song split. At least one album should contain multiple playable songs to prove album behavior.
5. **Publication:** publish accepted final assets to the in-app catalogue. A visitor can find the work and play it. The published version and its split are immutable for already recorded purchases; later edits create a new version. In human-review mode, publishing waits for administrator approval. In autonomous mode, an authorized agent publishes without a human click.
6. **Real revenue event:** a listener completes either a paid song/album purchase or a creator tip on testnet, with an order/tip ID, work ID, gross payment, platform fee if any, refund state, and final receipt. Both sources feed the correct work's revenue ledger. No payout from a play count or an unfunded CSV.
7. **Agent revenue split:** calculate exact integer entitlements from received funds and the committed split, then pay the agent recipients with retry-safe transaction tracking. A failed transfer remains owed; no successful payment is repeated. Human-review mode requires approval at the configured financial gates; autonomous mode lets a bounded agent execute them.
8. **Operator and listener views:** creators see drafts, tasks, versions, decisions, publication, sales, and balances. Listeners see the catalogue and purchase/playback state. The UI identifies demo/testnet data accurately.
9. **End-to-end proof:** in a browser, two or more agents make a playable song, publish it, a listener pays for it, and the resulting funds are split. Demonstrate both approval modes: one run pauses for the administrator and another completes automatically. Record final transaction and callback receipts; mocked inference or a manually funded reserve cannot substitute for this proof.

### Approval-policy behavior

| Mode selected by administrator | Creative and split proposal | Publish | Revenue distribution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Human approval required | Agents collaborate and prepare work, then wait at the configured gates. | Wait for administrator approval of the exact version and terms. | Wait for approval of the exact payable amount/split before execution. |
| Autonomous | Agents collaborate and resolve their own work within budget and policy limits. | Authorized agent can publish the accepted version. | Authorized agent can allocate and pay real received revenue within limits; failed or unknown outcomes remain pending. |

Changing the setting must create a new policy version and affect future actions only. It cannot retroactively authorize an old draft, alter a published work's historical split, or make an unknown payment safe to repeat. The administrator needs a way to pause or revoke future agent actions in either mode.

## Decisions that shape implementation

- **Revenue source:** paid song/album purchases and listener tips are both selected. The design still needs prices, tip minimum/maximum, asset, fee policy, refunds, and attribution when a tip targets an album rather than a song.
- **Autonomy implementation:** define the exact policy gates, per-agent authority, spending caps, versioning, emergency pause, and recovery behavior for both administrator-selected modes. An agent-owned account is distinct from a human's delegated personal assistant.
- **Music generation provider and rights:** choose an audio-generation service whose terms permit publishing the output in the catalogue. Record the actual service/model, input assets, output files, and any license constraints. Never claim rights or originality merely because an AI generated an audio file.
- **Payment asset and pricing:** select the testnet asset, song/album price, fee/refund policy, and recipients before changing the contract. Keep purchase principal separate from gas and operating expenses.
- **Media storage and moderation:** choose durable file storage, size/transcoding limits, malware/content checks, and a way to unpublish harmful or infringing material while preserving financial records.
